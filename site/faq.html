Questions players ask

The harder ones: how the game works out boss life, ailments, Vowbinding and the numbers in our tools. Everything here comes from the game's own data files and logs, and where we had to read between the lines we say so.

Bosses and Guardians

How much life does a Guardian have?

Four numbers multiply together:

  1. The Guardian's own base life: 820 for every Guardian.
  2. The monster level curve: 20 × e0.225 × level0.7. Monster level is the Rupture level up to 84 and stays at 84 after that. At 84 the curve is about 2,975.
  3. The Guardian multiplier: 1.5 + 0.06 per monster level, capped at 2.4, then halved. That's ×1.2 from monster level 15 on.
  4. The Rupture life multiplier above level 84. It climbs fast: about ×2.6 at Rupture 89, ×405 at 185, ×976 at 200, ×1,951 at 211, then about 6.5% more for each level after. These come straight from the game's "Zone rift" log lines.

Normal monsters use their own base life instead of 820 (25 is typical), magic monsters have ×2 and rares ×5. Gate and pinnacle Guardians use their own multipliers from the data. The planner and Damage Sim use these numbers for shock, and you can type in an exact life if you know it.

How do Guardians reduce my damage?
  1. Resistance. Monsters get 0.36% elemental resistance per level, up to 25%, so every Guardian from about monster level 70 has 25% fire, cold and lightning. A resistance variant adds +30% or +40% to one element, and Ward adds +25% (Rupture 60) or +35% (Rupture 80) to its own element. Penetration and curses take it back down, but only to 0%. Physical has no resistance.
  2. Mods it rolled (from monster level 40): Armoured is 23% less damage taken from everything; Elemental Ward is 23% less elemental damage taken and no ailments at all.
  3. Fight mechanics: Adaptive Ward gives it +40% resistance to the element you hit it with most, for 9 seconds every 18 or so.
A 1,000,000 lightning hit, Lightning +40% variant, 16% lightning penetration: resistance 25 + 40 - 16 = 49%, so 510,000 lands. With Elemental Ward: about 393,000.
Do curses and "takes more damage" effects stack on a boss?

Yes. Woe, Sentence, Hunter's Mark, War Banner and shock all multiply together. The game has a switch to count only the strongest one on bosses, but it's currently off.

Most curses work at half strength on a Guardian. Sentence is the exception and works fully.

Ailments

How big is a shock, and why is it smaller on bosses?

A shock makes the enemy take more damage, sized by how big your lightning hit is next to its life:

shock % = 50 × (lightning hit ÷ enemy life)^0.4, at most 20% increased shock effect raises both the size and the 20% cap (up to 50%) a shock under 5% (after increased effect) doesn't land

Each shock lasts about 2 seconds, and several shocks multiply: two 20% shocks are ×1.44, not ×1.40. Stacked shocks (Gathering Storm and the tree) add at most 60% in all. A normal monster you hit for 10% of its life gets a 20% shock. A Rupture 200 Guardian has 2.86 billion life, so even a 5 million hit is 0.17% of it: that's a 3.9% shock, which only lands with about 30% increased shock effect. Deeper Guardians shrug shock off completely, so Gathering Storm and shock supports stop being worth a socket there.

The game file lists 5% as the smallest shock. Players testing on high-level Guardians found shock doing very little, so the planner treats 5% as the point where a shock starts to land, not as a guaranteed minimum.

Shock isn't cut by resistance. It multiplies whatever gets through. Elemental Ward Guardians can't be shocked at all.

How do ignite, poison and bleed work?
  • Ignite: 6% base chance on hits with fire. It deals 90% of the fire hit over 4 seconds, and only the strongest ignite applies unless something lets you stack more.
  • Poison: 10% base chance on hits with poison. A "chance to poison" line also poisons with physical. Each stack deals 20% of the hit per second for 2 seconds, up to 40 stacks, and poison counts your damage-taken effects.
  • Bleed (Rend and bleed lines): 50% of the physical hit per second for 4 seconds, strongest one holds, 50% more while the enemy moves.
  • Damage over time multiplier (Deep Wounds and others) raises all three. Ailment duration lines make them last longer.

Hover the DPS number in the planner or Damage Sim to see how each one was worked out for your build.

Can I freeze or chill a Guardian?

Guardians use a freeze bar instead of normal freezing. Cold hits worth 12% of its life fill the first bar. A full bar freezes it for 1.5 seconds, then it's brittle for 4 seconds (10% more damage taken, or the shock if that's bigger). Each next bar needs ×1.5 more, and the bar empties after 6 seconds without a cold hit.

Chill slows by 10% to 30%, depending on how big the cold hit is next to its life (full at 10% of its life).

Vowbinding and reds

How do I put a Vow on a unique (red)?

In the Oathstone Cube, use Vowbinding: a Vow:

  1. Put in the red, a rare of the same slot, and runes.
  2. The first Vow costs a Tier V or higher rune. Every Vow after that costs red runes: 2 for the second, then 4, 6, 8, 10, 13, 17, 22, 28 and 36. Since the 7 November patch a red holds up to 11 Vows (166 red runes for all eleven).
  3. The red keeps everything and gains the rare's finest mod (the best tier first, never its Oathsworn line) as a Vow. The rare is used up.
  4. If the red already has a Vow of the same kind, the new one replaces it.

Each Vow counts for less than the one before: 100%, 85%, 70%, 60%, 50%, 40%, 33%, 27%, 22%, 18% and 15% for the eleventh. The game applies this live, in the order the Vows sit on the item (the tooltip shows "Vow 2 · 85%"), and rounds each reduced Vow to a whole number on its own line. So a +2 gem level Vow is still +2 in slot 2 (1.7 rounds up), and a +3 is still +3 at 85%. Put a gem level Vow second and give the full-strength first slot to a percentage line. The planner counts Vows the same way.

Idols: use Awakening (a magic idol + 2 jewels of Cut or finer) to turn a magic idol into a rare one, then feed that rare idol to a red idol with Vowbinding. Players have confirmed it works. Dwarven Runes take Vows too (confirmed in game): feed a red Dwarven Rune a rare Dwarven Rune, the same as any other slot. A rune only works in a Dwarven Rune Socket your tree has taken, so put each rune in an allocated socket. Plan it in Crafting (the Vowbinding bench), or add Vows to an item in the Build Planner's item editor.

How do I get a 7th line on a rare?

Use the Oathsworn cube recipe: a rare + a Tier V or higher rune + 3 Core Embers from your Forge store. The rare keeps every line and gains a 7th one, its Oathsworn line: a random mod it doesn't have yet, rolled at the best tier its item level allows.

Doing it again rolls the Oathsworn line again (the other six stay). A Vow never copies an Oathsworn line, so it only helps the rare itself, not a red you feed it to. Try it in Crafting, or add the line to a rare in the Build Planner's item editor.

Can a quiver get +2 to all gem levels?

Not as a normal rare: no quiver mod rolls gem levels. But a unique quiver can take it as a Vow. Vowbinding takes the finest mod of a rare in the same slot, and quivers share the off hand slot with tomes. A rare tome can roll +2 to Level of all Skill Gems (item level 44 and up), so a red quiver fed that tome gets +2 gem levels as a Vow.

The same trick works for any red: it can take any mod a rare of its slot can roll, even if its own base type can't. That's why some Vowbound items show lines you never see on a normal rare of that type. Gem levels above an attack gem's own level are 6% more damage each, so on attacks this is a solid Vow but not a huge one.

Can I move a unique onto a better base, like a Starfall Bow?

Yes, two ways:

  • Base Ascension: any gear (reds included) + a Tier III+ rune + 2 jewels of Cut or finer. It moves the item to the next base of its kind and keeps every mod, item level, socket and rune. Repeat it to climb, for example from a Stormwing Bow to a Starfall Bow.
  • Vowbinding: the Mark: the red + a Core Touched base of the same kind + a Tier V+ rune. The red moves onto the Core Touched base, which keeps its Mark as the implicit, and the item gets the higher item level. The Core Touched item's own mods are used up.

Both keep your Vows. In the planner, open a unique in the item editor and use Base to put it on any base of its kind, including Starfall and Core Touched ones. Its weapon damage, armour and so on follow the new base.

What else can the cube do with reds?
  • Red Turning: 2 reds of one tier + any rune give 1 random other red of that tier.
  • Red Unmaking: a red alone becomes 2 random jewels, finer for a higher tier.
  • Ascent, Reroll, Retemper and Echo also work on reds. See them all with their exact costs in Crafting.

Why doesn't attack speed make my Ballista fire faster?

Totems and ballistas fire on their own clock. The game's tooltip says it straight: "attack speed only places them faster". A Ballista fires every 0.7 seconds and a Spell Totem every 0.8. Only "fire faster" lines speed that up, and the number of totems multiplies the damage (up to 6 Ballistas or 10 Totems since the 7 November patch).

Do Trap and Skyfall work on a Ballista?

Yes, on this patch. With Skyfall socketed the Ballista counts as a Skyfall skill, so Skyfall nodes and Skyfall lines on gear count, and its bolts fall again: Twice-Fallen Sky, Twice Judged, the Falling Sky Dwarven Rune and the Dragonling's Final Judgement all add a second impact (players confirmed it in game on the 7 November patch). The planner adds them like the game: the biggest counts in full and the rest at half, up to one full extra impact (twice the hits). Skyfall's own 20% more damage is for spells only. Trap still places the skill, and it still falls.

Are physical increases on a weapon multiplicative?

No. A weapon's own lines add together: (base damage + flat added) × (1 + all % increased physical + quality). They look huge because Oathwords and Vows stack several big local lines. The planner's weapon card matches the in-game tooltip.

Are gem levels worth as much on attacks as on spells?

Not quite, and the first 5 gear levels count for more than the rest. On an attack, each of the first 5 levels from gear and passives above the gem's own level is 6% more damage; every level past that is only 1% more. On a spell, the first 5 grow the base damage about 19% each, then 16%, 13%, 10% and 8% for each level after that. Added damage from supports like Added Lightning grows with the gem's level and the first 5 gear levels, then about 4% a level. These fades come from the game's own settings file, so the planner and Damage Sim follow them.

Crit chance caps at 95%.

Why do my runes only give half?

Runes give half value in magic, rare and red items. Only a white (Normal) item gets a rune's full value, and only white items can form Oathwords. Four Othala in a red bow give +0.5 gem levels each, so +2 in total. Othala keeps its exact half; other runes round to the nearest whole number, with a half rounding up (Gar's 65% projectile damage gives 33%). All your gem levels add up first, then round, and a half rounds up. The planner counts runes this way.

How do Oathword power lines work?

An Oathword needs a white (Normal) base of the right kind with its runes socketed in order. Its fixed lines always apply, and its power lines depend on item level: each item level band picks a different set, so the same Oathword on an item level 84 base rolls bigger than on a 60. Lines of the same stat merge (60% and 152% show as 212%).

Do Fury and Bloodlust from one skill help my other skills?

Yes. Charges and Bloodlust stacks sit on your character, not the skill. Gather Fury on a clearing skill gives Fury (4% more damage and 4% faster per charge, up to 5) to everything. A Bloodlust support on a melee skill grants stacks from that skill's kills; each stack gives your melee attacks 0.5% increased damage and 0.2% attack speed. On a lone boss there are no kills, so Bloodlust only helps against packs, unless something like The Wolf's Due grants stacks from crits. Kindling is the spell version, and on a Guardian every 4% of its life the linked spell deals counts as a kill, so it does build up in a boss fight.

Defence

How is max hit worked out?

It's the biggest single hit your life plus energy shield can take, after everything that reduces it:

  • Resistances after the late-game -50% penalty (cap 75%, more with max resistance lines).
  • Armour against the physical part: armour ÷ (armour + 3 × hit), at most 80%. Armour reads any hit above 1,000 as 1,000, so it keeps working against big Guardian blows.
  • Evasion: you can't dodge a Guardian's big blow, but it glances: it deals less by your full evade chance (75% evade means 75% less, from the 10 November update).
  • Damage taken lines, and physical taken as elemental.

The Build Planner's Defences tab shows max hit for every damage type and lets you try a hit of any size.

Loot filter and tools

How does Cast on Crit work on attacks now?

Cast on Crit now fits attacks as well as spells, and does two different things there:

  • Attack with a spell on its link: the attack's crits cast that spell at the enemy struck, at most once every 0.15 seconds, for 25% less damage. This works like it always did for spells.
  • Attack with nothing on its link: the attack strikes by itself whenever your other attacks crit. That's at most once every 0.75 seconds, shared by every attack set up this way, for 25% less and then a further 44% less damage (so 42% of a normal use). A Rain of Arrows triggered this way drops one wave, not all three.

So a self-triggered Rain lands about 1.33 times a second at most, each one a single wave at 42% damage. That works out to roughly 15% of what a hand-cast Rain does, added on top of whatever you're actually pressing. It's a nice free extra when your main skill crits a lot, but it won't replace your main skill. The Build Planner counts it on your main skill's DPS and shows it as its own line.

This is how the playtest's files describe it. The main game's version only has the linked-spell part so far.

Can the loot filter show only energy shield bases, or rune bases with 3+ sockets?

Not yet. The game's filter rules can check item type (all chests count as one type), rarity, item level, which kind of mod line an item has, how good the rolls are, and how many good lines. They can't tell base types apart or count sockets. White rune bases with sockets always show from the Tidy preset up.

Why is my DPS different from the game's skill panel?

The game panel shows hits with no enemy. Our boss DPS is against a Guardian after its resistance, your curses, marks, shock and ailments. The sheet DPS in the planner should be close to the game's panel. If it's more than a few percent off, compare increased damage, added damage and crit first. Then tell us, with a screenshot, so we can fix it.

Some skills hit more than once per use, and the sims count that from the game's skill data. Flurry, Viper Fang, Thunderfall and Twin Cuts strike several times; the last strike of Flurry and Thunderfall is a heavier finisher, and the extra strikes make each use take longer, as the game's own timing code does. Thousand Cuts hops five times. Wheeling Axe hits on the way out and back. Sparks strike on their own clock while they last. Cyclone, Storm Whirl and Drain Life pulse on a timer that speeds up with attack or cast speed, and Storm Whirl also strikes one enemy each pulse for 45%. Unholy Void and Pyre of Faith tick twice a second. Orbiting Hammers hit once per hammer per turn, and Blade Vortex is counted with a full ring, like the game's panel. Winter Orb counts every icicle and its closing burst. Skills that leave burning ground count one patch at a time, because a recast replaces the old one. Skills with a cooldown can't be used faster than it comes back.

Thousand Cuts, the Sparks, Unholy Void, Orbiting Hammers and channel timing have been checked against real fights or the in-game panel. The rest follow the game's own data, skill text and code. If a skill looks off, your game log helps: it's at %LOCALAPPDATA%\BrokenOath\Saved\Logs\BrokenOath.log.
